Black hole lore: Einstein to right now

Two centuries from "dark stars" nobody believed in, to photographing the unseeable. Every date that mattered:

THE DARK STAR ERA · 1700s
1784

Michell's dark stars

English clergyman John Michell argues a star massive and compact enough would trap its own light — "dark stars" detectable only by gravity. Nobody can test it. The idea sleeps for a century.

1796

Laplace, independently

Pierre-Simon Laplace publishes the same idea in his Exposition — then quietly deletes it from later editions. The 19th century files it under "cute, unprovable."

RELATIVITY · 1915–1939
1915

Einstein rewrites gravity

General relativity: gravity isn't a force, it's curved spacetime. The equations permit extreme solutions — but even Einstein doubts nature actually builds them.

1916

Schwarzschild's solution — from the trenches

Karl Schwarzschild, serving on the Russian front in WWI, mails Einstein the first exact solution: a point mass whose gravity seals off a region of space. The math says the radius is 2GM/c². He dies of illness months later, aged 42.

1930–35

Chandrasekhar vs Eddington

19-year-old Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar calculates that dead stars above ~1.4 suns must collapse past white dwarfs. Arthur Eddington, the most famous astronomer alive, publicly ridicules him. Chandra is right; it costs him years.

1934

Supernovae forge neutron stars

Baade & Zwicky propose supernovae crush cores into neutron stars — the halfway house on the road to black holes.

1939

Oppenheimer: collapse never stops

Oppenheimer & Snyder show a heavy enough star collapses through its horizon in finite time ("frozen stars" to outside eyes). Published Sept 1, 1939 — the day WWII starts. Physics looks away for 20 years.

THE GOLDEN AGE · 1958–1974
1958–60

The horizon becomes real

Finkelstein identifies the Schwarzschild surface as a one-way membrane; Kruskal draws the full spacetime. The "frozen star" is reborn as something stranger.

1963 ×2

Kerr spins it · quasars blaze

Roy Kerr finds the rotating solution — the one real black holes use. Same year, Maarten Schmidt decodes quasar 3C 273: a galaxy core outshining trillions of stars. The engine? A feeding supermassive black hole.

1965

Singularities are inevitable

Roger Penrose proves collapse must make a singularity — no escape clauses. Black holes go from "possible" to "mandatory."

1967

A name at last (+ pulsars)

John Wheeler popularizes "black hole." Meanwhile Jocelyn Bell Burnell finds pulsars — spinning neutron stars, proof that ultra-compact corpses are real.

1971–74

Cygnus X-1 · the bet

Cygnus X-1's invisible companion (~15 suns) becomes the first widely accepted black hole. Hawking bets Thorne it isn't one (insurance for his own theories, he joked). He'd concede decades later.

1972–74

Black holes have thermodynamics

Bekenstein + Bardeen–Carter–Hawking: horizons have entropy and laws. Then Hawking radiation (1974) — black holes slowly evaporate. The information paradox is born and still bleeds today.

GIANTS IN EVERY GALAXY · 1990s–2000s
1994

Hubble weighs M87's monster

A spinning gas disk imaged at galaxy M87's core implies billions of solar masses in one spot. Supermassive black holes go mainstream.

1996

String theory counts the entropy

Strominger & Vafa reproduce Bekenstein–Hawking entropy from string microstates — a hint that spacetime is made of something deeper.

1990s–2000s

Our own monster, tracked star by star

Ghez and Genzel's teams map stars whipping around an unseen 4-million-sun mass: Sagittarius A*, 26,000 light-years away.

2004

Hawking concedes a bet

After 30 years, Hawking admits information might escape after all — paying off the Thorne–Hawking–Preskill bet with a baseball encyclopedia. (The paradox still isn't settled.)

WE SEE THEM · 2012–NOW
2012

The planet-sized telescope

The Event Horizon Telescope collaboration forms: link radio dishes across Earth into one Earth-wide eye, sharp enough to see a donut on the Moon.

2015–16

LIGO hears them collide

GW150914: two black holes (~36 + 29 suns) merge 1.3 billion light-years away, ringing spacetime. Announced Feb 2016. A new sense organ for the universe switches on.

2017

Gold from a kilonova

GW170817 — colliding neutron stars flash-forge heavy elements. Your jewelry is merger debris.

2019

The photo: M87*

April 10: a glowing ring around a 6.5-billion-sun shadow, 55 million light-years out. The most memed science image ever.

2020

Nobel hat-trick

Penrose (theory), Ghez & Genzel (Sgr A*) split the physics prize. Black holes, officially: real.

2021

M87*'s magnetic skeleton

EHT polarization maps reveal ordered magnetic fields at the edge — the fingerprint of matter being funneled and jetted.

2022

Our monster, imaged: Sgr A*

May 12: the Milky Way's own 4-million-sun giant, 2,000× smaller on the sky than M87* yet captured anyway.

2023

Baby giants break the models

JWST finds overgrown black holes in infant galaxies (UHZ1 and kin) — too big, too early. Formation theory scrambles to catch up. LIGO's O4 run starts flooding the catalog.

2024

Magnetism confirmed · a 33-sun neighbor

EHT sees polarized light spiraling around Sgr A* — same magnetic signature as M87*. And Gaia BH3 (33 suns) becomes the heftiest known stellar black hole in our galaxy.

NOW

Movies of doom, listen for space itself

ngEHT is being built to film black holes in motion. LISA (ESA, 2030s) will hear supermassive mergers across the universe. The EHT keeps staring — the next images will move.

Dates follow the published record; pop-history compresses a few. Sources worth your time: Thorne's Black Holes & Time Warps, the EHT papers (2019–2024), LIGO's GWTC catalogs, and the 2020 Nobel lectures.
