The minds behind the monster

The people who dragged black holes from "math artifact" to photographed reality:

1784

John Michell

Clergyman-geologist · England

First to reason that gravity could trap light itself. Right 130 years early; ignored for a century.

1915

Albert Einstein

Relativity · Berlin

Wrote the equations that allow black holes — then spent years doubting nature would use that page.

1916

Karl Schwarzschild

Astronomer-soldier · WWI front

Solved Einstein's equations from a trench, defining the non-spinning hole and its radius. Died the same year.

1930

Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar

Astrophysicist · Cambridge/Chicago

Found the mass limit dooming heavy stars to collapse; mocked by Eddington at 24, Nobel'd at 53 (1983).

1939

J. Robert Oppenheimer

Physicist · Berkeley

With Snyder, proved massive stars can't stop collapsing. Then the bomb ate the rest of his story.

1963

Roy Kerr

Mathematician · New Zealand

Cracked the spinning solution — the one every real black hole uses, including the render on your home page.

1965

Roger Penrose

Mathematical physicist · London/Oxford

Proved singularities are unavoidable, not artifacts. Nobel 2020. Still doodling impossible triangles.

1967

John Wheeler

Physicist · Princeton

Gave the beast its name — "black hole" — plus "it from bit" and a generation of relativists.

1967

Jocelyn Bell Burnell

Astronomer · Cambridge

Spotted pulsars as a grad student; her supervisor got the Nobel. The snub still stings astronomy.

1974

Stephen Hawking

Cosmologist · Cambridge

Showed black holes glow, leak, and die — wedding quantum physics to gravity and starting a 50-year paradox war.

1990s–2020

Ghez & Genzel

Observers · UCLA / MPE

Spent decades tracking stars around our galaxy's dark heart. Shared the 2020 Nobel for Sgr A*.

2019–

The EHT collaboration

300+ scientists · planet-wide

Led by Shep Doeleman, turned Earth into one telescope and took the picture. M87* first, Sgr A* next.
